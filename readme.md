Research Content Overview: This paper focuses on the core goal of "improving the targeted and effective interference of radars," and explores four major directions in research.
1  
White-box RGPO Interference Method for Single-Station Radar: Based on the theory of Random Simulation Optimization (SSO), a local simulation model of the radar tracking system is established. The interference strategy is optimized with simulated experimental data, solving the constraints of lacking target function analytical expressions, and achieving refined interference design in white-box scenarios.
2  
Black-box RGPO Interference Method for Single Station Radar: In scenarios where the radar tracking model is unknown and incomplete, a local sub-simulation model is constructed using multimodal estimation theory to fit the actual radar model, achieving a multimodal hybrid assessment of interference performance. Based on the classic SSO architecture (PSO-OCBA algorithm) and an improved SSO architecture (embedded active learning agent model), the efficiency of interference strategy optimization is enhanced, reducing computational burden.
3  
Optimization of Black-box RGPO Interference in SSO Architecture: Utilizing active learning agent models to address the computational resource competition issue in classic SSO architectures, specifically exploring and estimating candidate solutions. This paper proposes an efficient optimization algorithm to further enhance the efficiency of interference strategy optimization under incomplete information.
4  
Collaborative jamming method for networking radar: Based on the formation game theory of alliances, the task of multi-node collaborative jamming is transformed into a distributed optimization problem. A dynamic distributed task allocation algorithm is proposed, which achieves both the performance of centralized algorithms and the enhancement of computational efficiency while promoting network decentralization.
Conclusion The four types of radar jamming methods proposed in this paper effectively address the core bottlenecks of traditional jamming technologies: the white-box and black-box RGPO jamming methods have overcome design challenges caused by the absence of objective functions and incomplete information, achieving precise jamming for single-station radar tracking; improvements to the SSO architecture further enhance the optimization efficiency of black-box jamming; the networked radar collaborative suppression jamming method addresses the centralized limitations of multi-node task allocation, achieving dynamic distributed collaboration. Experimental validation demonstrates that all methods can effectively improve jamming effectiveness, significantly surpassing traditional UV-RGPO, UA-RGPO, and other jamming strategies as well as centralized and fixed task allocation algorithms. This lays a foundation for the refined and intelligent development of radar electronic interference, with significant practical implications for enhancing the battlefield survivability of aircraft.
